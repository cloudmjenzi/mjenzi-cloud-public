Data residency in Kenya, South Africa, Botswana and Australia: a practical guide

What the data protection laws in our four markets mean for where you host, and the questions to ask any cloud provider before you sign.

Mjenzi Cloud team · 6 November 2026 · 4 min read

"Where is my data?" used to be a question for regulated industries. Now it comes up in almost every cloud conversation we have, from banks in Nairobi to state agencies in Canberra.

This guide summarises what the main data protection laws in our four markets say about where personal information can be stored and processed, and what that means when you choose a cloud.

This is a plain-language overview, not legal advice. Laws and regulations change, and your obligations depend on your sector and contracts. Always check with your own legal or compliance team.

Kenya

Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 is overseen by the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC). Personal data can be transferred outside Kenya, but only with appropriate safeguards, such as adequate protection in the destination country, suitable contracts, or the individual's consent.

The Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021 go further for certain public-interest processing. Data processed for the strategic interests of the state, such as civil registration, elections, revenue administration and public health, must be processed through a server and data centre located in Kenya, or at least have a serving copy stored in Kenya.

What it means: government suppliers and anyone handling state-related data should plan for in-country hosting. Everyone else should document their cross-border safeguards.

South Africa

The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) has been fully in force since July 2021 and is enforced by the Information Regulator. Section 72 allows personal information to be transferred outside South Africa only if the recipient is bound by laws, binding corporate rules or an agreement that gives an adequate level of protection, or if another condition such as consent applies.

Sector rules can be stricter. Financial services firms, for example, must also meet regulator requirements on material outsourcing and cloud computing.

What it means: keeping data in South Africa removes a whole category of transfer risk, and simplifies audits for regulated industries.

Botswana

Botswana's data protection law was updated by the Data Protection Act of 2024, overseen by the Information and Data Protection Commission. Like Kenya and South Africa, it restricts transfers of personal data outside Botswana unless the destination offers adequate protection or other safeguards apply.

What it means: organisations in Botswana increasingly want a local option, especially public bodies, banks and telcos. In-country hosting in Gaborone keeps things simple.

Australia

The Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) apply to most businesses and to Australian Government agencies. Under APP 8, an organisation that discloses personal information overseas generally remains accountable if the overseas recipient mishandles it.

For government workloads, the Digital Transformation Agency's Hosting Certification Framework sets requirements for where and how government data is hosted, including ownership and control of the data centre and the provider. Security assessments under the IRAP program are common for sensitive systems.

What it means: onshore hosting is the default expectation for government and for many enterprises. For federal workloads, Canberra-based capacity close to agencies is a real advantage.

Questions to ask any cloud provider

  1. Exactly which countries and data centres will store and process my data, including backups, logs and disaster recovery copies?
  2. Can support staff or systems outside the country access my data? Under what controls?
  3. Which laws could compel the provider to hand over my data, including laws in the provider's home country?
  4. Can I choose the region for each workload and be sure nothing is replicated elsewhere without my consent?
  5. What happens to my data when the contract ends? How do I get it back, and how is it deleted?
  6. Which certifications and assessments does the provider hold, and can I see the reports?
